**Version:** v1.0.0 (theory-first)  
**Status:** Conceptual research figures; not experimental evidence  
**Date:** 2026-10-31

This index defines the eight standalone illustrations for the ISGFTR research repository. Each figure serves as a conceptual map and should be read together with the associated definitions, limitations, testable hypotheses, and research contracts. A diagram does **not** establish that a proposed mechanism has been empirically demonstrated.

## 2. Figure descriptions and scientific interpretation

### Fig-001 — ISGFTR Grand Architecture

**Target asset:** `figures/Fig-001-ISGFTR-Grand-Architecture.png`

**Intended elements:** capability profiles and boundaries; prospecting plane; experiment/probing plane; evidence and counterevidence registry; structural relay plane; governance and policy controls; feedback into subsequent questions and capability prospects.

**Interpretation:** The architecture is a proposed arrangement of research responsibilities, not a claim that an integrated runtime has been implemented. Distinguish candidate opportunities, observed results, and approved claims. Show evidence provenance and a failure/rollback route.

**Suggested insertion:** [ISGFTR-010](docs/ISGFTR-010-An-Open-Research-Architecture-for-Collective-Learning.md), after Section 3. Also suitable as the repository overview in [README](README.md).

**Suggested caption:** *Fig-001. Conceptual ISGFTR architecture connecting capability prospecting, controlled experiments, evidence adjudication, structural relay, and research governance. Feedback arrows denote research opportunities, not guaranteed intelligence growth.*

### Fig-002 — Four Intelligence Boundaries

**Target asset:** `figures/Fig-002-Four-Intelligence-Boundaries.png`

**Intended elements:** generative boundary (outputs available to a specified component); observed boundary (empirically supported capabilities); reachable boundary (capabilities accessible to a configured system); evolvable boundary (capabilities potentially accessible after future adaptation). Include task, resource, evaluator, and time qualifiers.

**Interpretation:** The four boundaries are **different questions**, not four universally nested circles. A diagram may use regions for pedagogy but must label them as schematic and avoid suggesting proven set inclusion or numerical scale.

**Suggested insertion:** [ISGFTR-001](docs/ISGFTR-001-From-Static-Intelligence-Boundaries-to-Open-Capability-Frontiers.md), after its boundary definitions; [ISGFTR-002](docs/ISGFTR-002-Five-Characteristics-of-Intelligence-Spillover-and-Relay.md), after Section 2; [ISGFTR-009](docs/ISGFTR-009-AGI-Implications-Limits-and-Counterarguments.md), after Section 3.

**Suggested caption:** *Fig-002. Four context-dependent capability boundary questions. The illustration does not imply that the boundaries are necessarily nested, complete, or directly measurable on one axis.*

### Fig-003 — Five Characteristics of Intelligence Flow

**Target asset:** `figures/Fig-003-Five-Characteristics-of-Intelligence-Flow.png`

**Intended elements:** spillover, generalization, fluidity, transfer, and relay as separate but interacting characteristics; arrows representing possible relationships; negative-transfer and failed-relay paths.

**Interpretation:** The five characteristics are analytical distinctions, not mutually exclusive causal stages or established universal laws. A system may show transfer without durable learning and relay without positive utility.

**Suggested insertion:** [ISGFTR-002](docs/ISGFTR-002-Five-Characteristics-of-Intelligence-Spillover-and-Relay.md), after Section 2.2 or before Section 8; optional overview in [ISGFTR-001](docs/ISGFTR-001-From-Static-Intelligence-Boundaries-to-Open-Capability-Frontiers.md).

**Suggested caption:** *Fig-003. Five distinguishable characteristics of intelligence flow and their possible interactions. Flow, successful transfer, and persistent growth remain separate empirical claims.*

### Fig-004 — Folding, Generalization, and Structural Transfer

**Target asset:** `figures/Fig-004-Folding-Generalization-and-Structural-Transfer.png`

**Intended elements:** source observations; folding into a reusable shared core; domain-specific residuals; context-aware unfolding into a target; probe, comparison baseline, and independent validation; optional uncertainty recovery.

**Interpretation:** Folding can support reuse but may discard critical structure. Successful unfolding requires context and validation. Preserve residuals and failed mappings; avoid a diagram that equates compression with generalization.

**Suggested insertion:** [ISGFTR-003](docs/ISGFTR-003-Folding-Generalization-Duality-and-Structural-Reuse.md), after Section 3 or 4; secondary in [ISGFTR-005](docs/ISGFTR-005-Directional-Intuition-and-Weak-Evidence-Transfer.md), after Section 4 or 5.

**Suggested caption:** *Fig-004. Hypothesized path from source observations to a reusable structural core and target-domain probes, with residuals, uncertainty, and validation retained explicitly.*

### Fig-005 — Directional Intuition and Selective Permeability

**Target asset:** `figures/Fig-005-Directional-Intuition-and-Selective-Permeability.png`

**Intended elements:** weak-evidence sources, structural analogies, OTHERS residuals, directional prioritization, Gate A exploration admission, bounded probe, Gate B evidence adjudication, separate deployment authorization, and counterevidence relay.

**Interpretation:** A directional score is an exploration-ranking signal unless separately calibrated; it is not a success probability. Admission to exploration is not authorization to claim or deploy a capability.

**Suggested insertion:** [ISGFTR-005](docs/ISGFTR-005-Directional-Intuition-and-Weak-Evidence-Transfer.md), after Section 8; [ISGFTR-004](docs/ISGFTR-004-Intelligence-Fluidity-and-Permeable-Structural-Boundaries.md), after Section 4 or 5; [ISGFTR-008](docs/ISGFTR-008-Intelligence-Spillover-and-Transfer-Prospecting.md), Sections 6–8.

**Suggested caption:** *Fig-005. Weak-evidence directional guidance through selectively permeable research boundaries. Exploratory ranking, claim validation, and deployment permission are independent decisions.*

### Fig-006 — System-Centric Multi-Level Learning

**Target asset:** `figures/Fig-006-System-Centric-Multi-Level-Learning.png`

**Intended elements:** execution units, interaction patterns, structural organization, relay memory, collective evidence registry, and possible evolutionary adaptation; learning controller outside frozen execution units.

**Interpretation:** Distinguish gains from static orchestration, learned routing, external memory, and internal parameter updates. System-level improvement does not establish model-weight learning or AGI.

**Suggested insertion:** [ISGFTR-006](docs/ISGFTR-006-System-Centric-Intelligence-and-Multi-Level-Learning.md), after Section 5 or 6; [ISGFTR-009](docs/ISGFTR-009-AGI-Implications-Limits-and-Counterarguments.md), after Section 5; [ISGFTR-010](docs/ISGFTR-010-An-Open-Research-Architecture-for-Collective-Learning.md), near Section 6.

**Suggested caption:** *Fig-006. Candidate learning loci across execution units, interactions, structures, relay histories, and collectives. The learning and execution mechanisms may be decoupled.*

### Fig-007 — Spillover Prospecting and Capability Frontiers

**Target asset:** `figures/Fig-007-Spillover-Prospecting-and-Capability-Frontiers.png`

**Intended elements:** known capabilities, open questions, weak evidence, candidate transfer directions, resource-bounded portfolio selection, controlled probes, validation, evidence registry, and revised capability claims.

**Interpretation:** The diagram depicts **search for potential**, not proof that a capability exists beyond an observed frontier. A prospect is a question, not an answer; failed probes remain valuable research outputs.

**Suggested insertion:** [ISGFTR-008](docs/ISGFTR-008-Intelligence-Spillover-and-Transfer-Prospecting.md), after Section 9; [ISGFTR-009](docs/ISGFTR-009-AGI-Implications-Limits-and-Counterarguments.md), after Section 7 or 13; [ISGFTR-010](docs/ISGFTR-010-An-Open-Research-Architecture-for-Collective-Learning.md), near Section 8.

**Suggested caption:** *Fig-007. Capability prospecting across uncertain frontiers: opportunity generation, prioritization, probing, evidence-based claim revision, and feedback into future research.*

### Fig-008 — Recursive Relay and Collective Intelligence Growth

**Target asset:** `figures/Fig-008-Recursive-Relay-and-Collective-Intelligence-Growth.png`

**Intended elements:** execution and observation; evidence collection; evaluation and counterevidence; provenance-preserving relay; structural or policy revision; retesting; retention or rollback; feedback to new prospecting. Show spatial and temporal relay.

**Interpretation:** Repetition may accumulate evidence even when it does not improve performance. Distinguish transmission, interpretation, adoption, utility, and durable growth. Recursive relay is not automatically recursive self-improvement or AGI.

**Suggested insertion:** [ISGFTR-007](docs/ISGFTR-007-Recursive-Relay-and-Open-System-Intelligence-Growth.md), after Section 5; [ISGFTR-002](docs/ISGFTR-002-Five-Characteristics-of-Intelligence-Spillover-and-Relay.md), near Section 7 or 8; [ISGFTR-010](docs/ISGFTR-010-An-Open-Research-Architecture-for-Collective-Learning.md), after Section 5 or 9.

**Suggested caption:** *Fig-008. Recursive evidence-bearing relay across systems and time. Durable growth is an outcome to be tested, with negative results, saturation, and rollback explicitly represented.*

## 4. Markdown insertion conventions

From a manuscript inside `docs/`, use a path **relative to that manuscript**, for example:

```markdown
![Fig-004. Folding, generalization, and structural transfer — conceptual model](figures/Fig-004-Folding-Generalization-and-Structural-Transfer.png)

*Fig-004. Hypothesized structural-core reuse and context-sensitive transfer; empirical validation remains necessary.*
```

From the repository root (`README.md`, `START-HERE.md`, or this index), use `figures/Fig-004-Folding-Generalization-and-Structural-Transfer.png` without `../`.

To avoid duplicated images, embed a figure at its main explanatory location and use text links or cross-references elsewhere. Preserve the exact figure IDs and filenames; use descriptive alternative text. Do not label schematic arrows as measured effects or represent unvalidated hypotheses as results.

- [ ] The final README, START-HERE, CONTENTS, and FIGURE-INDEX agree on IDs and filenames.

These figures are explanatory artifacts for an open, theory-first research program. They neither establish priority over existing research fields nor constitute validation of the ISGFTR hypotheses. Contributors are encouraged to propose corrections, alternative diagrams, negative cases, and better experimental representations. Refer to [TESTABLE-HYPOTHESES.md](TESTABLE-HYPOTHESES.md), [VALIDATION-ROADMAP.md](VALIDATION-ROADMAP.md), and the four specifications for claims and evidence requirements once those documents are published.
